● The dmesg output is the smoking gun. Those three lines all matched csi inside
  the word "SCSI" — they are false positives. There are zero real camera driver 
  messages in the kernel log. The firmware booted, ran auto-detection on the CSI
  port, found nothing, and loaded no camera overlay at all.

  camera_auto_detect=1 is correctly in place. The kernel just never saw any
  camera hardware.

  This is a physical connection problem.

  Check in this order:

  1. Is the ribbon cable actually plugged into the Pi's CSI/camera port? (not the
   display port — they look identical)
  2. Is the latch fully closed on both ends? — lift the brown/black latch, reseat
   the cable so it's fully inserted, then press the latch flat. A cable that
  looks seated can still be making no contact.
  3. Is the cable oriented correctly? — the silver contacts face toward the board
   on the Pi end, and toward the lens on the camera end (varies by camera
  module).
  4. Pi 5 has two camera ports (CAM0 and CAM1). If you're using CAM0,
  camera_auto_detect=1 should still work, but you can force it with:
  dtoverlay=imx708,cam0   # for Pi Camera Module 3
  # or
  dtoverlay=imx219,cam0   # for Camera Module 2
  4. Add the right one to /boot/firmware/config.txt after physically confirming
  the connection.

  After reseating the cable, run sudo dmesg | grep -iE 'unicam|imx|ov[0-9]' — if
  the camera is detected you'll see driver initialization messages there.
